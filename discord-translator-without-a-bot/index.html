A Discord translator without a bot

Updated 2026-11-02

When a translation bot is the right tool for a server, when it is not, and how to read any server in your language without asking an admin for anything.

Why most people reach for a bot first

Ask in any Discord help channel how to translate a server and the first answer is usually "add a translator bot". It makes sense: bots are how Discord grows new features, and there are many translation bots to pick from.

But a bot is a tool for the server, not for you. Whether that is what you need depends on one question: are you the one running the server, or a guest in it?

How a translation bot works

A bot is an account that lives inside a server. To get one there, someone with the right permissions in that server has to add it. After that, it usually works in one of these ways:

  • On request. Someone reacts to a message with a flag, or types a command, and the bot posts the translation as a new message in the channel.
  • Linked channels. The admin connects, say, a Spanish channel and an English channel, and the bot copies each message across in the other language.
  • Per member. Members tell the bot their language, and it sends them translations.

Every one of these needs the server to set it up, and most of them put translated copies where other people can see them. Some translator apps can also be added to your own Discord account instead of a server, and then appear in the Apps menu when you right-click a message. That avoids the admin, but it is one message per right-click.

When a bot is the right call

To be fair to bots: if you run a community with members who speak different languages, a bot can be exactly right. It gives everyone the same translated copy, members who never installed anything can read along, and you control which channels it touches. If that is your situation, a bot is a sensible choice, and Subline is not trying to replace it.

When a bot is the wrong tool

Most people who search for a translator are not running the server. They are guests:

  • You joined a Japanese art server, a Brazilian gaming community, or a study server where half the chat is in Korean.
  • You are in many servers, and each would need its own bot.
  • You do not want to ask an admin for anything, or announce that you cannot read the language.
  • You want to follow a fast chat without anything extra posted in it.

In those cases you want a translator that works on your side of the screen, for you alone.

What reading without a bot looks like

Subline runs inside your own Discord, not inside the server. Picture a channel in a Portuguese speaking server:

  • rafa: alguém sabe se o evento de hoje foi adiado?
  • bia: foi pra amanhã, mesma hora
  • rafa: valeu!!

On your screen, each line keeps its original, and a translation appears under it, marked ✦: "does anyone know if today's event was postponed?", then "it moved to tomorrow, same time", then "thanks!!". Rafa and Bia see nothing different. No message was added, nothing was posted, and nobody had to approve anything.

Side by side

A translation bot:

Questions people ask

Can the server tell I am using it?

No. Subline reads what Discord already shows you and draws a line under it on your screen. It never sends anything into the chat.

Do I need the server owner's permission?

No permission from the server is needed, because nothing is added to the server. Discord's own terms are a separate question: Subline is built on Vencord, a client mod, and the safety page explains what that means plainly.

Will it translate what I write back?

No. Subline translates what you read, not what you send. Many people find that fine: you can follow the chat, and reply in English or in a few words of the language you are learning.

Does it work for DMs?

Yes, when you choose. A direct message stays untranslated until you hover a message in it, press 🌐 and choose Enable auto-translate here. That way a private conversation is never sent anywhere unless you decided so.
