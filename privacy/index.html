Privacy

Last updated 19 October 2026

Subline translates Discord messages on your computer. To translate, it must send text to a translation service. This page says what leaves your computer, where it goes, what is kept, and for how long. It describes what the current version (0.2.8) does today. Where something is outside our control, or we have not confirmed it, this page says so.

The short version

  • Subline translates nothing until this computer has a plan. That is a linked Discord account with a plan. A plan from before 0.2.8 keeps working on this computer until a Discord account on it links. Without a plan, no Discord message text leaves your computer.
  • Linking uses Discord's own Authorize window. It asks only for identify. The relay keeps a keyed hash of your Discord account id and the day your Discord account was made. It never keeps your Discord account id, your username or your email. See Linking your Discord account.
  • ≈ translations go to Google. Your computer sends the message text straight to Google Translate.
  • Names and pronouns go to Google only when you hover them, or open a profile card. Only names in a script your language does not use, and pronouns. See Names and pronouns.
  • ✦ translations go to Subline's relay, then to an AI service. Each ✦ request also carries usernames, message ids and up to 8 earlier messages for context. Those earlier messages can include your own.
  • The relay does not store the messages it translates. For ✦ previews only, it keeps a scrambled form of the text for up to 3 days, so the same message is not counted twice. It keeps your code, your account, the keyed hash from linking, scrambled ids of your computers, and purchase links. Most of these have no expiry.
  • Feedback goes only when you press Send. You see all of it first. The relay keeps it for 180 days and emails a copy to the owner. See Feedback box.
  • Payments go through Dodo Payments, and start only inside Discord. This website sells nothing. Dodo collects your name, email and payment details. Dodo's payment notices to the relay can include your name, email, address and some card details. The relay does not store or log them.
  • Subline checks in with the relay every time Discord starts, and about every 24 hours. It sends your install id. Only uninstalling stops this.
  • No ads and no cookies. No third-party analytics or tracking scripts in the app or on this website. This website sends Subline's server one ping when the home page opens, and one when you press a download button. The relay keeps only daily counts of these, and its own usage counts (see Stats and This website).
  • Your computer keeps copies. Translations, your code (in plain text), a log file and settings stay on your computer. Some of these stay after you uninstall.
  • You can turn things off. Use the globe button for one channel, or Subline → Settings for all servers and for profiles and embeds. DMs are off until you turn one on.

Messages and ≈

The ≈ translation comes from Google Translate. Subline uses it on every plan.

When a message is sent

  • Only when this computer has a plan: your linked Discord account has one, or this computer had one before 0.2.8 and no Discord account on it has linked yet.
  • Only in the channel you have open. Other channels send nothing until you open them.
  • Only in channels that are on. Server channels are on by default. DMs and group DMs are off by default.
  • When you open a channel, Subline translates up to 20 recent messages. This is the Earlier messages setting. You can set it to 0.
  • When you scroll up and Discord loads older messages, Subline translates up to that number of them again.
  • New messages and edited messages are translated as they arrive.
  • Embeds, polls and forwarded messages follow the same channel rule.
  • Messages from bots and from people you have blocked are treated like any other message. Subline does not filter them out.

What is not sent

  • Your own messages are never sent to be translated by themselves. If you press ⚡ on one of your own messages, it is sent. With ✦, your own messages can also go as context. See the next section.
  • Messages that need no translation stay on your computer. This includes text that is only emoji, mentions, links, digits or punctuation. It also includes chat shorthand, laughter and repeated words. Subline decides this on your computer.
  • If you read English, a message that Subline is sure is English also stays on your computer. For other reading languages, Subline cannot tell this on your computer. The message goes to Google, and with AI to the relay, and they answer that it needs no translation.
  • Language detection runs on your computer. Google also detects the language as part of each translation. There is no separate detection request.
  • Attachment file names and voice audio are never translated or sent.

What Google receives

  • The message text, the language to translate from, and your reading language.
  • The "from" language is usually "auto". For a short reply, Subline may use the language of the message it replies to.
  • Mentions are replaced by names before sending. A user mention becomes that person's server nickname, display name or username. Channel and role mentions become their names. Custom emoji are removed.
  • Google does not receive the author's name, message ids, server or channel ids, or earlier messages.
  • Your computer sends the request directly to Google. Google therefore sees your IP address.

Technical details

  • Address: https://translate-pa.googleapis.com/v1/translate. This is the service behind Google's own translate widget. Subline uses that widget's public key.
  • The text travels inside the web address of a GET request. One request goes per message, or per piece of a long message. Text that would make the address longer than 14,000 characters is split into several requests.
  • In the Discord app, the request identifies itself as Chrome 130 on Windows, with translate.google.com as its referrer and origin. It carries no Discord or Subline identifier.
  • In the Discord app, Discord's background process on your computer makes the request, not a web page.
  • In the browser add-on, the add-on's background part makes the request. A browser does not let an add-on set these headers, so the request carries your browser's own User-Agent, with no translate.google.com referrer or origin. Google may see that the request came from the Subline add-on. The request carries no Discord identifier.
  • Timeout: 15 seconds. If Google says "too many requests", Subline tries once more after 0.4 seconds. When Google is the only translator in use, it retries up to 3 times, after 0.4, 1.5 and 4 seconds. A batch that fails with a network or server error is retried once after 1 second.
  • With ✦ active, each message goes to both Google and the relay.

Subline does not control what Google keeps. We do not know how long Google keeps the text or your IP address. Google's own privacy policy applies.

✦ AI translation

The ✦ translation comes from an AI model. Your computer does not talk to the AI service directly (one rare exception is under Older versions). It sends the request to Subline's relay, a small server program on Cloudflare at relay.subline.page. The relay checks your code, then asks the AI service.

When ✦ is used

  • Automatically, on every message that ≈ would translate, when your plan includes AI.
  • With AI, when you press ⚡ on a single message. This works on any message, in any channel, DMs included. It sends only when you press it.
  • For ✦ previews. Automatic owners without AI get up to 5 a day. Press ⚡ on a single message, in any channel, DMs included. A preview is sent only when you press it.

What one ✦ request carries

  • Up to 25 messages. For each: the message text (with names put in for mentions, as with Google), the Discord message id, and the author's Discord username. The username is the account handle, not the display name.
  • Reply information. If a message is a reply, the id of the message it replies to. If that message is not in the same request, its author's username and its first 200 characters also go.
  • Context lines. Up to 8 earlier messages from the same channel, as username and text, so the AI understands the conversation. ⚡ and previews send up to 6. Your computer cuts each line to 600 characters. For a ✦ preview of a message up to 4,000 characters, it does not, and the relay cuts each line to 1,000 characters before the AI service sees it. Context lines carry no ids.
  • Your reading language.
  • Who is asking. Your Subline code if this computer has one, your install id, the Subline version, the app it runs in (the Subline app's version, or the add-on and its version), and a version number of the request format. See The install id.

Context includes messages that are not translated. Subline keeps the last 64 messages of each open channel in memory. Context lines are picked from these. They can include your own messages, messages already in your language, shorthand, and messages from bots and from people you have blocked. Subline does not filter any of these out of the context.

Rare cases

  • A message over 4,000 characters is sent in parts. Each part carries the message id with a part number.
  • If putting names in for mentions makes a message longer than 4,000 characters, Subline may send Discord's raw text instead. Raw text shows mentions and custom emoji as Discord's own codes. Those codes include user, channel, role and emoji ids.

What is not in a ✦ request

  • No Discord user ids, except in the rare raw-text case above.
  • Not your Discord account id, and nothing from linking. Translation requests never carry your Discord account id.
  • No server id and no channel id.
  • No email address and no name from your purchase.

Which AI service receives it

  • The relay sends the request to OpenRouter, a service that passes requests on to AI hosts. The model is openai/gpt-oss-120b.
  • Subline asks OpenRouter to try Groq, then Cerebras, then DeepInfra, in that order.
  • Subline also allows OpenRouter to fall back to other hosts of the same model. So another host can serve a request. Subline excludes four hosts: Novita, DigitalOcean, SambaNova and Amazon Bedrock.
  • Since 19 October 2026, Subline asks OpenRouter for zero data retention and no data collection. OpenRouter then sends a request only to hosts that, under their terms with OpenRouter, do not keep prompts and do not train on them. Groq, Cerebras and DeepInfra are all on that list.
  • If OpenRouter fails, the relay sends the request to Groq directly.
  • A long request may be split into parallel pieces. Each piece carries all the context lines.
  • The relay's code can also use Google Gemini. That path is switched off today.

What the AI service receives

The relay turns your request into one text prompt. The prompt holds the context lines as "username: text", each message as "[id] username: text", a note for each reply, and the target language.

  • It does not hold your code, your install id, or your IP address. The AI service sees the relay's Cloudflare address, not yours.
  • OpenRouter receives two labels that say the request comes from Subline: the address https://subline.page/ and the name "Subline".
  • Subline sends no per-user identifier to the AI service.
  • The zero-retention rule above is OpenRouter's list. We have not checked each host ourselves. OpenRouter's own policy applies to OpenRouter. When the relay goes to Groq directly, Groq's own policy applies.

What the relay keeps from a ✦ request

  • Message text: not stored. The relay's code does not save or log message text, usernames or request bodies.
  • A daily number per code: how much ✦ you used today, to apply daily limits. The row holds your code and that number, and no text. For ✦ previews, it holds your Subline account id instead of a code. That is not your Discord account id. The row is deleted within 3 days.
  • A per-minute count, held in memory only.
  • For ✦ previews only: a marker so the same message is not counted twice. It holds a 96-bit scrambled form (SHA-256, no secret) of the message id, target language and message text, under your account. A long message sent in parts gets one such marker per part. It also gets a few markers for the whole message, named with a scrambled form of its message id and target language (no text), that hold its part sizes and what was charged. All are deleted within 3 days.
  • Stats markers and an analytics row. See Stats.
  • The day your computer was last seen, on your account. See Your code and account.

Limits: AI plans allow about 2,000 ✦ messages a day and 60 requests a minute. Each message counts 1. A request with long text or long context counts 1 more for each 1,000 characters above 1,000 per message. The relay refuses a request above 40 messages or 131,072 bytes. It keeps only the newest 12 context lines, cuts each to 1,000 characters, and keeps at most 6,000 characters of context in all. It does not translate a message over 4,000 characters.

Profiles, statuses, embeds and titles

With a paid plan, Subline also translates text around messages. The setting is Profiles, embeds and more in Subline → Settings. It is on by default.

  • Bios and custom statuses of other people, wherever Discord shows them: member list, profiles, the DM list and DM profiles. These have no channel or DM switch. They are translated even when DMs are off. Your own bio is never sent. Your own status is not sent from your profile, but it can be sent when it shows in a member list.
  • Embeds, polls and forwarded messages, in channels that are on.
  • Channel topics, voice channel descriptions and voice statuses, guidelines and events, in server channels that are on.
  • Server rules, and events that belong to no channel. These have no channel switch.
  • Stage topics, thread titles, forum post titles and forum tags, in server channels that are on.
  • Server onboarding questions and answers. These have no channel switch.
  • Reply quotes. Subline reuses an existing translation. It sends a new request only when the quoted message is not loaded and the channel is on. It never sends one for a blocked, ignored or suspended author.

With Automatic, these go to Google. With AI, they go to Google and to the relay. A request for these carries the text and your reading language. It has no username, no Discord ids and no context. A text over 2,000 characters is not sent. Longer kinds, such as embed descriptions, are cut to 4,000 characters. ✦ use for these is limited to 200 units a day.

Names and pronouns

With a paid plan, Subline also translates people's names and pronouns. The setting is Names and pronouns in Subline → Settings. It is on by default. Without a plan, no name is sent.

  • What. Display names, server nicknames, usernames and pronouns, where Discord shows them: message authors, mentions, profile cards and reply previews. Never in the member list, the DM list or voice channels. Never server, channel or role names.
  • When. A name is sent only when you rest the pointer on it, or when you open that person's profile card. Nothing is sent when the pointer only passes over a name.
  • Which names. Only a name written in a script your reading language does not use, such as 王小明 or Дмитрий for an English reader. A name in your own script is never sent by itself. Names in fancy fonts are read on your computer first. Emoji, symbols and digits do not count. With AI, a name in a script no language uses (runes, say) is never sent to Google: it goes to the relay only when you press ✦.
  • Which pronouns. People can write any text in the pronouns field, so it follows the profile text rule: pronouns with a letter go to Google when you open the profile card, and nothing shows when they are already in your reading language.
  • Where. To Google, like profile text. A request carries the name or pronouns and your reading language. It has no username, no Discord ids and no context.
  • ✦. With AI, you can press ✦ on a name or on the profile card line to ask the relay for it. Only then does it go to the relay and an AI service, like a profile ✦. It counts toward the same 200 units a day.
  • Names and pronouns have no channel or DM switch. The setting turns them all off.
  • Translations of names are kept in the profile and embed cache on your computer, with the original name. See What stays on your computer.

Vencord's settings file

  • Mac: ~/Library/Application Support/Vencord/settings/settings.json
  • Windows: %APPDATA%\Vencord\settings\settings.json

Subline's section of this file holds:

  • Your Subline code, in plain text. It is not encrypted. Anyone who can read your files can read it. Subline does not use the system keychain.
  • The codes of other Discord accounts on this computer, in plain text. When another Discord account signs in, Subline puts the code of the account that signed out aside (accountCodes), with that account's removed and rejected codes, under its short local key. So each account keeps its own code. They stay until you uninstall with "Also remove my settings and code".
  • Which Discord account the saved code belongs to (codeAccount), as that account's short local key.
  • Your install id (installId). Until a Discord account links it, every Discord account on this computer uses it.
  • Which Discord account linked that install id (installBinding), and which accounts sent a link whose answer has not arrived yet (installPendingBind). Each account is a short local key, so the link stays with that account if Discord's own data is cleared. The key is made from the account on this computer. It is not the Discord id itself, and it is never sent anywhere.
  • A code you removed by hand, so it is not saved again.
  • A code the relay rejected, and when.
  • Your reading language and your settings.
  • Hidden fields from older versions, such as personal AI keys if you once entered any.

In the browser add-on, the same fields are kept in your browser's storage for discord.com (localStorage, as VencordSettings), not in a file.

The log file

  • Mac: ~/Library/Logs/Subline/subline.log
  • Windows: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Subline\logs\subline.log
  • The Subline app, its background helper and its uninstaller all write here.
  • It holds versions of Subline, Discord and your system, what the app decided and why, and error causes. The helper also copies the launch times from status.json. It can hold full folder paths, which include your computer user name.
  • It never holds message text, your code or your install id. Fields named like text, message, key or token are blanked before writing.
  • Each file is rotated before 512 KiB. 3 older files are kept, so at most 4 files (about 2 MiB) exist.
  • Copy diagnostics in the Subline app puts the current log and the newest older log on your clipboard. It replaces your home folder path with ~. Nothing is uploaded. You choose whether to send it to us.

The background helper

  • Mac: a login item at ~/Library/LaunchAgents/com.subline.helper.plist. It runs at login, every hour, and when Discord's files change.
  • Windows: a scheduled task named \Subline\Helper. It runs at sign-in and every 5 minutes, as you, with the lowest rights.
  • It repairs Subline after a Discord update, puts back missing Subline files, and installs Subline updates. If it does this while Discord is open, a notification asks you to quit and reopen Discord. It never closes Discord itself. Its notifications are local. Its only network use is the update check under Background checks.

The install id

  • What it is. A random code of 32 letters and digits (16 random bytes). It names this Subline install. It contains nothing about you or your computer.
  • When it is made. The Subline app makes it when it installs Subline. If none exists, Subline makes one inside Discord. An existing one is never replaced.
  • One per Discord account. Until a Discord account links the install id in settings.json, every Discord account on this computer uses it. The account that links it keeps it. Every other Discord account on this computer then gets its own install id, made inside Discord. When you switch Discord accounts, Subline switches install ids with you.
  • Where it is kept. In Vencord's settings.json as installId, and in Discord's storage. Subline does not show it on screen.
  • Who receives it. The relay, with nearly every request from Subline inside Discord: translation, status checks, linking, checkout, server codes, patch reports and feedback. The Subline app never sends it. Google never receives it. The log file never holds it.
  • With it. Every request from Subline to the relay also names the Subline version and the app it runs in: the Subline app's version, or the add-on and its version. The app version comes from app-version.json in Subline's folder.
  • Why. The relay joins each install id to your linked account, so your plan works on every computer and browser where you link the same Discord account. It also lets a purchase made from Subline switch on by itself.
  • Kept after uninstall. Uninstall keeps it even when you tick "Also remove my settings and code". This is on purpose. A reinstall then joins your account again by itself.

The scrambled form

The relay usually stores a scrambled form instead of the id itself. It is the first 16 characters of a SHA-256 hash of the id (with a fixed prefix). No secret is added to the hash.

  • The id is random and long. So nobody can work back from the scrambled form to the id by guessing.
  • But the scrambled form is the same everywhere. It works as a stable pseudonym for your install. The same value appears in your account, purchase links, checkout records, server code claims from older versions, patch reports and the pass the relay gives Subline. For older versions it also appears in stats markers.
  • When you buy from inside Subline, this scrambled form also goes to Dodo Payments with the checkout. See Payments.
  • A few relay rows hold the id itself, not the scrambled form. These rows exist for older versions only. See Older versions.

Linking your Discord account

Every plan belongs to one Discord account. You link that account once on each computer, and once in each browser. Then your plan works there too.

How it works

  • Subline opens Discord's own Authorize window, inside Discord. It asks only for identify. Subline has no Discord bot.
  • With identify, Discord can tell Subline your account id, username, display name, avatar, banner and a few account details, such as your Discord language. It does not give your email. Subline cannot read your messages, servers or friends through it, and cannot post anything.
  • Before anything is linked, Subline shows the Discord account the plan will belong to, and asks you to confirm. The name and picture Subline shows before a link come from Discord on your computer. They are never sent.
  • When you press Authorize, Discord gives Subline a one-time code. Subline sends it to the relay, with your install id, like other relay requests. It also sends the id of the Discord account that is signed in, so the relay can check that Discord authorized that same account. If not, nothing is linked. The relay only compares the two ids. It never stores or logs the one Subline sent.
  • The relay gives the code to Discord and gets a short-lived access token. It asks Discord who you are and takes only your account id. It never keeps the token. Subline then stays in your Discord settings under Authorized Apps, so your other computers link by themselves. You can remove it there at any time.
  • The relay never stores or logs your Discord account id, the one-time code, the tokens, your username or your email. Its log lines about linking hold no account data. At most they hold Discord's answer status.
  • If you press Cancel, nothing is sent and nothing is stored.
  • The "Linked to" name in Subline → Settings comes from Discord on your computer. It is not sent anywhere.
  • Translation requests never carry your Discord account id.

What the relay keeps

  • A keyed hash of your Discord account id, on your account and as a lookup row that leads to your account. It is made like the keyed hashes under IP addresses, with the relay's secret key and its own label. Without the key, nobody can work back from it to your Discord account id.
  • The day your Discord account was made. The relay works it out from the account id. It is used to check that a Discord account is at least 30 days old before it can claim a server code.
  • Both have no expiry, like the account. They are kept per linked account, never per computer.
  • If the key is missing on the relay, linking is switched off and the relay stores nothing from it.

Many computers

  • A linked account has no computer limit. A computer not seen for 90 days drops off the account's list. It joins again by itself the next time it checks in.
  • When one linked account is used on 15 or more computers in 30 days, the relay writes a flag for the owner to look at. It holds the random account id, the number of computers and the day. Nothing is blocked. The flag has no expiry.
  • Two Discord accounts on one computer link one by one. One account's plan never applies to the other. An account without a plan is told which account on this computer has it. That name comes from this computer only.

If Discord's Authorize window is missing

A Discord update can move the Authorize window. Then Subline opens Discord's Authorize page in your web browser instead.

  • After you press Authorize, Discord sends your browser to the relay with the one-time code. The relay keeps the code, the scrambled install id of the computer that asked, and a keyed hash of its network address (IPv4 in full, or the first 64 bits of IPv6). It uses them to give the code only to that computer.
  • If your browser is on another network, the page shows a 6-digit code. You type it in Discord.
  • All of it is deleted at most 5 minutes after the last check. The code is used once.
  • Subline on your computer asks the relay every 2 seconds, for up to 5 minutes, whether the code has arrived. Each check carries your install id, like other relay requests.

Move my plan

  • Only the Discord account that holds the plan can move it, from Subline → Settings, under Plan. The new account must Authorize too. A plan moves at most once every 30 days.
  • To start, the relay gives a one-time ticket. It keeps the ticket for 10 minutes, with your random account id and the keyed hash of your Discord account id.
  • When the new account authorizes, the relay moves the codes and the plan to that account. If that account has its own plan, the two are joined. Linking the new account works as above.
  • On both accounts, the relay keeps when a plan last moved. On the old account, it keeps a list of the codes that moved away (the newest 50), so its computers stop using them quietly. For 30 days it keeps a marker under the keyed hash of each of the two Discord accounts, so the plan cannot move again sooner.
  • The owner page lists each move: the two random account ids and the day. The relay keeps the newest 200.

Lost Discord account

Write to support. The owner can unlink your old Discord account from your plan. The keyed hash of its id is then deleted from your account. Your codes and grants stay. Then link your new Discord account and enter your code from your receipt email.

Your code and account

Codes

  • A code you buy is the license key that Dodo Payments issues. Other codes usually start with slp_: codes from a server code, for early users, or made by the owner.
  • The relay keeps a record for each code: its status, plan, daily limit, a short note, when it was made, when it expires, and Dodo's payment or subscription id.
  • Code records have no expiry. After a refund, a lost dispute or the end of a subscription, the code is marked revoked. The record stays.
  • Checking a code before you confirm it does not link it to your computer and writes nothing.
  • A code entered on a linked account joins that account. A code that belongs to another linked Discord account is refused. Write to support in that case.

Account and computers

  • When you link, or when a code is first used, the relay makes an account. The account has a random id. It links your codes and your computers.
  • The account holds your codes, the scrambled ids of your computers, the day each computer was last seen, the Subline version and the app version each computer last reported, which computers bought AI, notes such as a server code you redeemed or an early-user grant, and, once linked, the keyed hash of your Discord account id and the day that account was made.
  • A computer that had its plan before 0.2.8 keeps it until a Discord account on it links. The account notes the scrambled ids of those computers.
  • A linked account has no computer limit. See Linking your Discord account.
  • Older versions (0.2.7 and earlier) do not link. For them, a code works on up to 3 computers, and a computer not seen for 30 days can be replaced by a new one.
  • The last-seen day is written at most once a day per computer.
  • Early users: a computer that used Subline before a fixed early-user date receives Automatic at no charge. The relay keeps a marker for that computer, with no expiry, so this happens only once.
  • The account and its links have no expiry.

On your computer

Your code is shown in Subline → Settings, under Your code. You can select it and copy it. It is saved in plain text in Vencord's settings.json.

Payments

Dodo Payments sells Subline as the merchant of record. You start a purchase inside Discord, in Subline → Settings. You pay on Dodo's checkout page. You enter your card details there, not in Subline. This website sells nothing.

What Dodo collects

Dodo collects your name, email, billing details and payment details on its own page. Dodo emails you your code. Dodo decides what it keeps, and for how long. Dodo's privacy policy applies.

What Subline sends to Dodo

  • From inside Discord: the relay asks Dodo for a checkout. It sends the product, a quantity of 1, a return address (https://subline.page/?from=discord), and the scrambled install id. Nothing else: no name, email, code, Discord account id or IP address.
  • Before it makes a new checkout, and after a refund, the relay may ask Dodo how your recent checkouts stand. That request carries only the checkout id.
  • If the relay cannot be reached, Subline opens Dodo's checkout link directly. That link also carries the scrambled install id.
  • Subline only opens checkout links on dodopayments.com.
  • The Subline app (the installer) starts no purchase.

What Dodo sends back

  • Payment notices to the relay. When you pay, get a refund or open a dispute, Dodo sends the relay a notice. Going by Dodo's published API, these notices can include your name, email, phone number and billing address. Payment notices can also include card details: the cardholder name, the card's country and its last four digits. The relay receives them. It reads only the event type, product, payment and subscription ids, status, renewal date, whether a refund is partial, license key, scrambled install id and checkout id. It does not store or log your name, email, phone, address or card details.
  • The return address. After payment, Dodo sends your browser back to subline.page. Dodo adds your license key and your email to the address. See This website.

What the relay stores about a purchase

  • No expiry: your license key as your code, and Dodo's payment id or subscription id, linked to it.
  • 30 days: a link from your scrambled install id to your code, so Subline can switch on by itself.
  • 3 days: notes that a purchase is in progress, links from Dodo's payment or subscription id to your scrambled install id and to the checkout, the purchase result (paid, pending or failed), and changes from payment notices waiting to be matched to a code.
  • About 2 days: the checkout session, linked to your scrambled install id. Recent checkouts per computer, with their Dodo link, product, where they started and when.
  • 2 hours: how many checkouts your computer and your IP address started this hour. The limits are 6 per computer and 20 per IP address. The IP counter's name holds a keyed hash of your address, not the address (see IP addresses).

Waiting for payment

While a checkout is open, Subline asks the relay every 5 seconds for 30 minutes. Then it asks every 5 minutes, for up to 48 hours. Each check sends your install id, as every status check does.

Server codes

A server code is a short code (4 to 16 letters and digits) that a Discord server shares. It gives Automatic.

  • You need a linked Discord account that is at least 30 days old.
  • Redeeming sends the code and your install id to the relay.
  • The relay makes a new slp_ code for you, notes the server code on it, and links it to your account. These have no expiry.
  • Each server code keeps a list of the scrambled install ids that claimed it. No expiry.
  • Each Discord account can claim one server code, ever. The relay keeps a marker for that, under the keyed hash of your Discord account id. No expiry. Older versions kept this marker per install instead.
  • If you use IPv6, the relay counts claims of that server code from your network (the first 48 bits of your IPv6 address). This limits how many claims one network can make. It stores a keyed hash of the network, not the network itself (see IP addresses). A network's count is deleted 30 days after its last claim of that server code. Nothing is stored for IPv4.
  • Tries from your IP address are counted per day: at most 30 successes and 20 failures. The counter's name holds a keyed hash of your IP address (IPv4 in full, or the first 64 bits of IPv6), not the address. It holds only counts. It is deleted 2 days later.
  • When a server code hits a network limit, the relay logs the server code and a short tag made from the keyed hash.
  • When a server code is claimed quickly (half or nine tenths of its claims within an hour), the relay logs the server code and claim counts.
  • The relay counts, per server code, how many accounts that redeemed it later bought AI. This is a number only, with no expiry.

Keyed hashes

  • A keyed hash is HMAC-SHA-256 with a secret key that only the relay holds. The relay keeps the first 32 hex characters.
  • Without the key, nobody can work back from the hash to your address, even by trying every possible address.
  • Each use mixes in its own label, so the same address gives a different hash in each place.
  • If the key is missing on the relay, it stores nothing made from your address. It then refuses patch reports and linking. Website pings and install check-ins are then not counted at all. Server code claims run without the network limit and without the per-address limit on tries. Feedback runs without its per-install and per-address limits. Checkouts, purchase checks and older versions run without their per-address limits. The other limits still apply.
  • Server code network counts stored before 0.2.4 held the plain first 48 bits. The relay converts each one to the keyed form the next time that server code's record is loaded. Without the key, it deletes them.

Background checks

Some requests happen without you doing anything. None of them carries message text.

Status checks (Subline inside Discord)

Subline asks the relay what your plan is. It sends your code (or, without one, your install id), your install id, the Subline version, and the app it runs in. If this computer used Subline before 0.2.0, it adds a flag that says so. It runs:

  • every time Discord starts, even if you never bought anything;
  • again 24 hours after the last check (Subline looks at the clock every 60 seconds);
  • after your computer wakes from sleep (a gap over 5 minutes);
  • 60 seconds before the saved answer expires;
  • when Discord's window comes back online or into focus, at most once every 10 minutes;
  • when your code changes;
  • for Automatic without AI, when ⚡ first shows on a new UTC day;
  • after AI ends, at 2 minutes, 10 minutes, 30 minutes and 2 hours;
  • after the relay refuses a ✦ request for your plan, and after you redeem a server code or try to buy something you already own;
  • after a failure, at 5, 15 and 60 seconds, then every 5 minutes until answered.

Each check shows the relay that your install is in use, when, and with which version. Cloudflare sees your IP address. If this computer or your code is on an account, the check updates this computer's last-seen day there. A status check never carries your Discord account id.

The relay also counts how many installs check in each day, and how many of them are new. See Stats.

Patch reports

Discord updates can break the parts of Discord that Subline hooks into. Subline checks for this once per Discord session: 30 seconds after messages first load, or after 10 minutes if they never load.

  • A report is sent only if something broke. A clean check sends nothing.
  • At most one report is accepted per Discord release channel, Discord build and UTC day. If the relay refuses a report's format, Subline sends it once more with only the items an older relay knows. If the relay cannot take it, Subline tries again the next time Discord starts.
  • It contains: the Subline version and build, Discord's release channel (stable, ptb, canary or other) and build number, and up to 20 broken items. Each item is the name of a Subline part, the search text Subline uses to find its place in Discord, and the kind of failure. It is at most 8,192 bytes. It contains nothing about you.
  • It goes with your install id. It runs on every plan, also before you activate.
  • The relay keeps, for 8 days: the scrambled install id, a keyed hash of your IP address (see IP addresses), Discord's channel and build, and the broken items. It does not keep the Subline version or build.
  • When 3 or more installs from 3 or more IP addresses report the same break on one UTC day, the relay emails the owner. The email holds the count of installs, the day, the broken item, and the Discord builds affected. It holds no install id, hash, IP address or anything about you. The number of these emails is capped per day.

Update checks (background helper)

  • The helper downloads a small release file from GitHub: https://github.com/subline-page/subline/releases/latest/download/subline-release.json.
  • It checks at most every 6 hours, or sooner when Subline is broken or missing.
  • If there is a newer version, it downloads it from GitHub. It accepts only GitHub addresses. It checks the file's size and SHA-256 before use.
  • These requests carry no install id, no code and no custom headers. GitHub sees your IP address.
  • The helper never contacts the relay.

The Subline app

The Subline app (the installer) never contacts the relay. It only installs. It writes your reading language and a new install id into Vencord's settings.json. Linking and your plan happen inside Discord. The app does not update itself. It has no crash reporter and no analytics.

The browser add-on

Subline can also come as an add-on for Discord in your browser: Chrome, Edge, Brave and Opera through the Chrome Web Store, and Firefox through Firefox Add-ons. It is not out yet. This page will say when it is.

  • It runs the same Subline inside Discord in your browser. It sends the same kinds of requests as Subline in the Discord app, as this page describes: ≈ text to Google, and ✦ requests, status checks and linking to the relay.
  • The add-on's own background part sends these requests, only to relay.subline.page and Google's translate service.
  • The add-on also has access to discord.com pages. It uses it for one thing: it removes Discord's Content-Security-Policy headers from those pages, so Subline can run inside Discord. Vencord's own browser add-on does the same. It reads nothing and sends nothing through this access.
  • You link your Discord account in the browser once, the same way. Your plan then works there.
  • It keeps its data in your browser, like the app keeps it in Discord's storage.
  • It has no background helper, no status file and no update check of its own. Your browser's add-on store updates it.
  • Removing the add-on stops everything it sends.

Vencord badge list

Subline runs on Vencord, an open-source Discord mod. Subline removes most of Vencord, but keeps its required badge feature.

  • When Discord starts, and then every 30 minutes, Discord downloads https://badges.vencord.dev/badges.json. This list names Vencord's own badges.
  • When you open a profile that has such a badge, Discord loads its picture from the address in that list.
  • These requests carry nothing from Subline. Vencord's server sees your IP address.
  • Subline has no setting to turn this off.

Feedback box

The feedback box is at the bottom of Subline → Settings. A message that shows a Subline translation also has Report translation on its right-click menu. That opens the same box, with that message attached.

What you can send

  • Your message, up to 2,000 characters. You need a message or a report.
  • Your email, if you want a reply. It is optional. Leave it empty to stay anonymous.
  • A bad-translation report, only from Report translation. It holds that one message's original text, the translation you saw, whether it was ≈ or ✦, and the two languages. It never holds other messages. The original is usually someone else's message. Mentions, channels, roles and custom emoji in it are replaced by words such as "@user" and "#channel", so it holds no Discord ids.
  • Recent error codes. Up to 10 from this Discord session. Each is a code from a fixed list, with its time to the minute and, if there was one, an HTTP status number. No error text and no message text.
  • App details. The Subline version and build, your system (Mac, Windows, Linux or other) and your plan.

The report, the error codes and the app details each have a tick box. They are ticked by default. Untick one, and it is not sent.

Nothing is sent by itself

  • Opening the box sends nothing. Review shows every field exactly as it will be sent. Only Send sends it.
  • It goes to the relay at relay.subline.page. Like every relay request, it carries your install id. Cloudflare sees your IP address.

What the relay keeps

  • The fields you sent, an id, the time to the minute, whether the email copy went out, and whether and when the owner marked it handled.
  • No IP address, no install id, and no scrambled form of either.
  • Each item is kept for 180 days. A daily cleanup then deletes it.
  • To apply the limits below, the relay keeps daily counters. Each counter's name is a keyed hash of your install id or of your IP address, mixed with the day (see IP addresses). They hold counts only. The first daily cleanup after that UTC day deletes them.

Limits: 5 items a day per install, 10 a day per IP address (IPv4, or the first 64 bits of IPv6), and 200 a day from everyone together. A request may be up to 49,152 bytes. Each text in a report may be up to 4,000 characters. At most 50 email copies go out a day. After that, items are stored without a copy.

The email copy

  • For each item, the relay sends one email to the owner's personal mailbox, hosted by Gmail. It goes through Cloudflare's email service (Email Routing), from alerts@subline.page.
  • The copy holds everything you sent, the item id and the time to the minute.
  • If you gave an email, it is the copy's reply-to address. So a reply from the owner goes to you. Replies currently come from the owner's personal Gmail address.
  • Copies stay in that mailbox. We have not set a deletion schedule for them.

Who can read it

  • The owner. Stored items are read through the relay with an admin key held by the owner. Email copies are read in the owner's mailbox.
  • Cloudflare stores the items. Google stores the email copies.

Stats

  • Daily totals. The relay counts per day: active paid codes, ✦ previews served, and purchases per plan. For older versions it also counts active installs and new first-use records. These are numbers only. They are kept 400 days.
  • Install counts. The relay counts how many installs check in each day (see status checks). It also counts how many of them are new: an install it has never seen before. For this, it keeps only scrambled markers, never your install id. Each marker is named with a keyed hash of your install id (see IP addresses). The day's marker is kept 2 days. The "seen before" marker is kept 400 days. The counts are numbers only, kept 400 days.
  • Checkout counts. The relay counts per day how many checkouts start, for Automatic and for AI. It counts new purchases the same way. These are numbers only, kept 400 days. A purchase with a 100%-off coupon is not counted as a purchase. To leave it out, the relay keeps Dodo's payment id of a payment of 0 for 3 days.
  • Website counts. See This website.
  • "Seen today" markers. To count each paid AI code once a day, the relay writes a marker named with a 16-character SHA-256 fingerprint (no secret) of your code. For older versions, the fingerprint is of your install id instead. It is written on ✦ requests. It is kept 2 days.
  • An analytics row per ✦ request, in Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine. It holds the result (for example "ok" or "daily limit reached"), an 8-character fingerprint (SHA-256, no secret) of your code, your install id or, for ✦ previews, your account id with a fixed prefix, the plan name, the number of messages, and sometimes the AI service's error code. It holds no text, no IP address and no country. Cloudflare decides how long it keeps these rows.

Server logs

  • Cloudflare's Workers Logs keep only the relay's own log lines. Request logs (each request's address, headers and network details) are switched off.
  • The relay's own log lines are warnings and errors with fixed text. They contain no message text, no usernames and no request bodies. Error text is cut to 200 characters. On the translation, spending and plan-check paths, codes, install ids and IP addresses are blanked from error text. Other error text is not blanked.
  • Some log lines do hold: a server code with a short network tag or claim counts, Dodo checkout and payment ids, a Dodo product id, up to 200 characters of an error from Dodo, broken item names from patch reports, a feedback item's id when its email copy fails, and the AI service's name and error status.
  • Cloudflare's network still carries each request. It can keep its own security and traffic records, which can include your IP address. Cloudflare decides what it keeps, and for how long.
  • Subline's setup adds no other log store.

This website

  • This website sells nothing. All buying happens inside Discord.
  • subline.page is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub receives each visit, with your IP address.
  • No cookies. No local storage. No third-party analytics, scripts, fonts or images.
  • Pings to Subline's server. The home page sends one ping to the relay when it opens. It sends one more when you press a download button. The privacy page sends none.
  • A ping holds the source: the page's ?ref= value, or the name of the site you came from (for example reddit.com, never the full address). A download ping also holds the computer type of the button: Mac or Windows.
  • A ping sets no cookie and holds no id. The relay keeps no IP address, no browser details and no id from it.
  • The relay keeps only daily counts: page opens, and download presses for Mac and for Windows, each also per source. It keeps up to 50 source names a day. Other sources count as "other". The counts are kept 400 days.
  • To limit fake counts, the relay counts at most 30 page opens and 10 download presses per IP address a day. The counter's name holds a keyed hash of your address, not the address (see IP addresses). It is deleted after 2 days.
  • The download section asks GitHub for the latest release. Your browser calls https://api.github.com/repos/subline-page/subline/releases/latest. The answer is kept in your browser tab (session storage) for 10 minutes. Downloads come from GitHub.
  • The page guesses your system (Mac or Windows, and the Mac chip type) inside your browser. The guess is never sent. A download ping says only which button you pressed: Mac or Windows.
  • When the browser add-on is out, its buttons link to the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons. Those stores then see your visit.

Returning from checkout

  • After a payment started in Discord, Dodo sends you back to subline.page. The address includes your license key, your email, the payment or subscription id, and a status.
  • Your browser sends this full address to GitHub's servers when the page loads. We do not know if GitHub logs it. Browser extensions you use may also see it.
  • The page then removes these values from the address bar and the browser history.
  • The page shows your code so you can copy it. It never reads your email. It never sends your code anywhere.
  • If the payment is still pending, the page asks the relay for the result. It sends only the payment or subscription id, every 4 seconds for up to 3 minutes. It stops after 3 failures in a row. The relay answers with the state only. It keeps that state for 3 days.

Support email

  • Mail to support@subline.page passes through Cloudflare Email Routing.
  • It is forwarded to the owner's personal mailbox, hosted by Gmail.
  • Replies currently come from that personal Gmail address.
  • Support mail is kept in that mailbox. We have not set a deletion schedule for it.

Debug log

  • Debug log in Subline → Settings is off by default.
  • When on, Subline writes details to Discord's developer console: the text of messages it skipped, translations as they are saved, and message ids for each request.
  • This stays on your computer. Subline does not upload it.
  • When it is off, the console gets only a few lines with no message text. A few of them name a message id or channel id.

Older versions (0.1.x)

Versions before 0.2.0 worked without a code. They send your install id to the relay in place of a code. If you still run one, the relay also keeps:

  • A first-use record that holds your install id itself (not scrambled), for 90 days.
  • Daily counts per install, holding the install id itself, for 2 days. Per-minute counts, for 2 minutes.
  • Daily counts per IP address, for 2 days. The counter's name holds a keyed hash of the address (IPv4 in full, or the first 64 bits of IPv6), not the address.

The relay reads first-use records to recognise early users.

Versions 0.1.8 and earlier let you enter your own AI key (for Groq, Anthropic or Google Gemini). That key stays hidden in Vencord's settings.json. If it is still there and no Subline code is saved, ✦ requests go from your computer straight to that AI service, with your key. They carry the same message text, usernames, message ids and context lines as a ✦ prompt, and that service sees your IP address. Saving a Subline code switches this off.

Your choices

  • Turn off what you do not want translated. See Turning things off.
  • Send feedback only if you want to. Leave the email empty to stay anonymous, and untick any part you do not want to send.
  • Records on the relay are kept for the times under Retention at a glance. Records marked No expiry stay.
  • To remove the link between your Discord account and your plan, write to support@subline.page. On request, support can unlink your Discord account: the keyed hash of its id is deleted from your account. Your codes and grants stay on the plan.
  • To have your data on the relay deleted, write to support@subline.page. The owner deletes your account, its codes and its links by hand. Deleting your codes and account ends your plan. Dodo keeps its own records of a purchase.

Remove what is on your computer

  1. Uninstall in the Subline app, and tick "Also remove my settings and code".
  2. Delete the logs: ~/Library/Logs/Subline on Mac, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Subline on Windows.
  3. The install ids are in two places: Vencord's settings.json and Discord's storage. If you remove both, the relay sees this computer as a new one. Linking again joins it to your account.
  4. Data inside Discord's storage stays until you delete Discord's app data folder.
